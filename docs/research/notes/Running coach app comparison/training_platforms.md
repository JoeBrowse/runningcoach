## Q1. What are the current prices and free tiers?

### Takeaway
The cheapest are Intervals.icu (free, Supporter $4/mo), Runalyze (free, Premium €60/yr) and Final Surge (free for athletes). Strava ($79.99/yr) and SportTracks ($59/yr, no free tier) sit in the middle. TrainingPeaks Premium ($134.99/yr) and Stryd Membership ($129/yr, on top of a pod purchase) cost the most. Strava + Runna is sold as a $149.99 / £119.99 annual bundle. Runna is NOT included in a standard Strava subscription.

## Q2. What analytics models does each platform use (Banister, TSS, rTSS, power)?

### Takeaway
Most of these platforms use a Banister-style impulse-response model: exponentially weighted chronic load (fitness), acute load (fatigue) and their difference (form). The input differs: TSS/rTSS/hrTSS on TrainingPeaks, power/HR/pace load on Intervals.icu, Relative Effort on Strava, TRIMP on Runalyze, and RSS/Critical Power with Running Stress Balance on Stryd. Final Surge was reported to lack a PMC-style load model.

### Cited Findings
- TrainingPeaks: TSS is the base metric. The Performance Management Chart shows Fitness (CTL), Fatigue (ATL) and Form (TSB ≈ fitness minus fatigue). The PMC is Premium-only and shows as "Coach Only" for Basic users. Performance Insights (Sep 2026, Premium) labels Form bands and Fitness ramp rates and adds Recovery/Training insights from HRV, resting HR, sleep and RPE. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- TrainingPeaks: running TSS/PMC values can be wrong if the threshold is not kept up to date. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- Intervals.icu: fitness, fatigue and form tracking plus power curve and HR analytics on the free tier. Submaximal fatigue tests are a Supporter feature. — [intervals.icu/pricing](https://www.intervals.icu/pricing)
- Stryd: Critical Power (CP) is the foundation for training zones and workout targets. Running Stress Balance (RSB) is the primary load metric, showing whether a runner is fatigued, recovering or ready to push. Stryd Assistant flags when RSB may be unreliable (gaps, missing data) and detects CP drift, recommending a calibration workout rather than auto-adjusting CP. — [the5krunner, 3 Jun 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/06/03/stryd-assistant-adaptive-training/)
- Runalyze: tools include a TRIMP calculator and Effective VO2max, plus a "Performance monitor" and performance curves. — [runalyze.com/pricing](https://runalyze.com/pricing)
- SportTracks: lists "Performance prediction" and per-sport tools. — [sporttracks.mobi/pricing](https://sporttracks.mobi/pricing)
- Final Surge: the reviewer did not find a PMC with CTL/ATL/TSB curves or any load modelling on the website or help centre. — [Coachbox](https://coachbox.app/en/compare/final-surge-review/)
- Strava: Instant Workouts derive pace targets from estimated fitness, such as a 5K time inferred from history. DC Rainmaker found this estimate was polluted by a GPS-glitch run and did not update after the run was deleted. — [DC Rainmaker, 13 Jan 2026](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/01/stravas-instant-workouts-actually.html)
- Strava has in-app "Performance Predictions". Strava warns Claude-computed figures may differ from them. — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-now-lets-runners-talk-to-their-training-data-through-claude/)

### Inferences
- **[BK]** TrainingPeaks rTSS uses Normalized Graded Pace relative to functional threshold pace. hrTSS uses HR relative to threshold HR. Default CTL and ATL time constants are 42 and 7 days (the Coggan/Allen adaptation of Banister).
- **[BK]** Intervals.icu computes "training load" from power, then HR, then pace (configurable priority). CTL/ATL use 42/7-day exponential averages by default, and the time constants are user-configurable. It also models eFTP / critical power, W′ and pace curves, and supports GAP-based pace zones.
- **[BK]** Strava "Fitness & Freshness" (subscriber) is driven by Relative Effort, an HR-zone-weighted TRIMP-like score, or power-based Training Load for cyclists. It is a Banister-style chart.
- **[BK]** Runalyze uses TRIMP (Banister) for ATL/CTL/TSB. It adds "Marathon Shape" (readiness for marathon based on weekly mileage and long runs) and race-time prediction from Effective VO2max (Daniels VDOT-like tables corrected for HR).
- **[BK]** Stryd's Running Stress Score (RSS) is power relative to CP. RSB is a short-minus-long-term load balance (Banister-like). The Race Power Calculator predicts race power and time from CP.
- **[BK]** SportTracks has a "Fitness / Fatigue / Form" chart built on a TSS-like training load, plus performance prediction.
- For an open-source app: the Banister/PMC family is the de facto standard runners expect, and it is unpatented and easy to implement. Proprietary names (TSS, rTSS, TrainingPeaks trademarks) should be avoided.

### Gaps
- Exact current formulas or time constants for Strava, SportTracks and Runalyze were not verified in 2026 sources.
- Whether Final Surge added any load model in 2026 is unknown. The reviewer found none.

## Q3. Platform profiles: top complaints, praise, unique features, and what users would miss

### Takeaway
Strava is the social hub, but it is a weak coach. Its AI and Instant Workouts are criticised as shallow, and Runna is still a separate paid app. TrainingPeaks is the coaching industry standard, but it is pricey, has no AI coach, and is now owned by Garmin (July 2026). Intervals.icu is the most capable free analytics tool with an open API, but it is web-first. Stryd has the most sophisticated adaptive running-specific plans (CP/RSB-based) but needs hardware. Final Surge is the free-for-athletes coach platform popular with US running coaches. Runalyze is a deep free running analytics site. SportTracks is a niche paid log.

### Cited Findings
**Strava**
- Strava acquired Runna in April 2025 (price undisclosed). The apps remain separate with separate subscriptions. — [the5krunner, Oct 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/28/runna-vs-strava/); [DC Rainmaker, Apr 2025](https://dcrainmaker.com/2025/04/strava-acquires-runna-thoughts-forward.html)
- Integration so far: Strava race discovery uses Runna's race database, and Runna was one of 14 integrations in Strava's May 2026 strength overhaul (sets/reps/weight/muscle maps). — [search summary](https://www.t3.com/active/strava-2026-future-and-challenges); [9to5mac, 21 May 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/21/strava-adds-dedicated-strength-training-support-for-sets-reps-weight-and-muscle-groups/); [WHOOP press](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/press-center/strava-overhauls-strength-experience-with-expanded-partner-ecosystem-new-workout-log-and-muscle-maps/)
- Instant Workouts (Jan 2026, subscribers only): weekly refresh each Monday, four categories (Maintain, Build, Explore, Recover) with five workouts each and a suggested route per outdoor workout. Watch push was not available at launch (promised "soon" for Garmin/Apple). Readers reported it live but rough by early Feb 2026 (unverified). — [DC Rainmaker, 13 Jan 2026](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/01/stravas-instant-workouts-actually.html)
- DC Rainmaker's criticisms: bad pace targets from a corrupted 5K estimate; poor routes (technical trails for intervals); a 2.5-hour "Recovery Ride" with tempo blocks; confusing wording; no indoor/outdoor distinction; instructions not visible mid-workout. He called it "media fodder" ahead of the IPO. His praise: route generation is strong and Strava's historical data is a big advantage. — [DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/01/stravas-instant-workouts-actually.html)
- Athlete Intelligence has reportedly moved from novelty AI summaries towards explaining effort, fatigue and trends in plain language. — [T3](https://www.t3.com/active/strava-2026-future-and-challenges)
- Strava–Claude MCP connector (launched June 2026, all paying tiers): read-only access to full activity history, per-second HR/pace, GPS, power, clubs/events. Capabilities: fitness trends, readiness, goal planning, cross-sport analysis, gear. Unpublished rate caps. Claude only at launch. — [Marathon Handbook, 3 Jun 2026](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-now-lets-runners-talk-to-their-training-data-through-claude/)
- Strava has 195M+ users in 185+ countries. — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-now-lets-runners-talk-to-their-training-data-through-claude/)
- Strava confidentially filed for an IPO (draft registration, confirmed 2 Feb 2026). — [TechRepublic, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-strava-api-scraping-crackdown/)
- Praise: segments, multisport, route discovery, a free tier good enough for logging. Criticism: leaderboards and pace comparison push runners to run easy days too fast. Duplicate uploads happen if both Runna and the watch upload. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/28/runna-vs-strava/)

**TrainingPeaks**
- Garmin acquired TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic (announced 22 Jul 2026, terms undisclosed). This raises open questions about continued support for COROS/Polar/Suunto. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- Premium-only features: self-planning, Annual Training Plan, unlimited workout libraries, PMC, 30+ trend charts, Peak Performances, elevation correction, calendar sync, weather, Fueling Insights, TrainingPeaks Virtual (indoor riding). Plans and coaching are sold separately. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- No AI coach found. The only "AI" is race bots in TrainingPeaks Virtual. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- Marketplace sells plans for running, triathlon, cycling, swimming, rowing, winter sports and strength. TrainingPeaks' own figures: 100k+ monthly marketplace visits, 70k+ plans sold in 2023. Dynamic Training Plans push dated workouts to many athletes at once. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- Ratings as of 22 Sep 2026: US App Store 4.7 (~12.7k ratings), Google Play 4.2 (~33.4k reviews). — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)
- Praise: the industry standard for coaching, TSS as the shared vocabulary, the widest device support. Complaints: no AI coaching, short refund window, inconsistent pricing across pages, threshold-dependent TSS accuracy. — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)

**Final Surge**
- Free-for-athletes model with flat coach pricing. Marketplace sellers include Greg McMillan, Runner's World UK, Tinman and Jeff Galloway. Coaches keep 70% of plan sales. — [Coachbox](https://coachbox.app/en/compare/final-surge-review/)
- Structured workouts can mix pace/HR/power targets and sync to Garmin Connect, Apple Watch, TrainerRoad, Zwift and Wahoo; the blog also mentions COROS, Suunto, Stryd and Amazfit. - Weaknesses: no load modelling or PMC, no AI assistant, US-centric with an English-only help centre, mixed "Classic"/"Beta" UI. Daily vitals don't sync from Garmin/Polar (Apple Health, HRV4Training, Elite HRV or manual entry instead). — [Coachbox](https://coachbox.app/en/compare/final-surge-review/) (written by a competitor; treat critique cautiously)

**Intervals.icu**
- Full free analytics including fitness/fatigue/form, interval detection, workout builder and calendar. Supporter adds an MCP AI connector and an annual plan builder. — [intervals.icu/pricing](https://www.intervals.icu/pricing)

- Zwift training API integration has been announced. — [Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/?p=112599)

**Stryd**
- Stryd 5.0 / Duo 5.0 pods and Adaptive Training were announced 7 Oct 2025. Adaptive Training launched in Nov 2025. — [Stryd blog](https://blog.stryd.com/2025/10/07/introducing-stryd-5-0-stryd-duo-5-0-and-stryd-adaptive-training/)
- Stryd Assistant (2 Jun 2026) has eight features:
  - Recap: plain-language summary after each structured workout.

  - RSB accuracy flag.

  - Body Status Tracking: injuries, illness and menstrual cycle. Flagged injury or illness reduces load.

**Runalyze and SportTracks**
- Runalyze offers AI assistants via MCP and many analytics features free. It is EU-based with EUR pricing. — [runalyze.com/pricing](https://runalyze.com/pricing)
- SportTracks: performance prediction, workout planning with calendar sync, PR filtering, weather, customizable layouts, coaching. One $59/yr plan. — [sporttracks.mobi/pricing](https://sporttracks.mobi/pricing)

## Q4. How open are their APIs (important for a free open-source integrator)?

### Takeaway
Intervals.icu is by far the most integration-friendly: a full documented REST API with OAuth or API keys, webhooks, and workout write-back. Strava has become steadily more restrictive. In Nov 2024 it limited display to the owning user and banned use of API data in AI models. In June 2026 it began requiring a paid Strava subscription for Standard-tier developers, dropped intermediary platforms, and told AI users to stop pending written guidance. Strava is a risky primary data source for a free AI coaching app. TrainingPeaks' API is partner-gated and is now under Garmin. Runalyze and SportTracks have API docs but limited scope. Final Surge and Stryd show no public API.

### Cited Findings
- Strava, Nov 2024 API agreement (effective 11 Nov 2024): third-party apps may display a user's Strava data only to that user. It "explicitly prohibit[s] third parties from using any data obtained via Strava's API in artificial intelligence models or other similar applications". It also adds protections for Strava's look and feel. Strava claimed fewer than 0.1% of apps were affected. — [DC Rainmaker, Nov 2024](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/11/stravas-changes-to-kill-off-apps.html); [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-users-cant-publicly-share-data-third-party-apps-311329); [the5krunner clarification, 20 Nov 2024](https://the5krunner.com/2024/11/20/strava-issues-clarification/)
- Strava, June 2026: new Standard-tier developers needed a Strava subscription from 1 Jun 2026, and existing ones from 30 Jun 2026 (reported $11.99/mo, varying by region). Extended Access developers are not affected. Public profile and club data are moving behind login. Apps that route data through third-party intermediaries are no longer supported. Developers using API data in AI models should "stop and seek written guidance from Strava before continuing." Inference-only LLM use (no storage or training) is unresolved, per a 7 May 2026 forum thread with no public answer. Strava's developer count grew to 241k from 185k. — [TechRepublic, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-strava-api-scraping-crackdown/)
- Strava's own AI path: a first-party Claude MCP connector for paying subscribers (read-only, unpublished rate caps). Strava is "looking to support other AI clients in the future". — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-now-lets-runners-talk-to-their-training-data-through-claude/)
- Intervals.icu: full REST API to upload/download activities, manage wellness, create workouts and receive webhooks. OAuth 2.0 and API-key auth. 200+ third-party apps integrated. — [intervals.icu features/extend](https://www.intervals.icu/features/extend/)
- Intervals.icu Supporter adds an official MCP connector for your own AI assistant. — [intervals.icu/pricing](https://www.intervals.icu/pricing)
- Intervals.icu Strava sync: once planned to become paid to cut API calls, then reversed to free for all. This shows Strava-API cost pressure on downstream platforms. — [Intervals.icu forum](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/strava-sync-available-to-all-users/33397); [Wahoo forum](https://wahoox.forum.wahoofitness.com/t/intervals-icu-strava-sync-becoming-paid-feature/21812)
- Runalyze links API documentation and offers AI assistants via MCP. — [runalyze.com/pricing](https://runalyze.com/pricing)
- SportTracks' footer links a "Developers" API docs section (/api/doc). — [sporttracks.mobi/pricing](https://sporttracks.mobi/pricing)
- Final Surge: the review did not mention any API. — [Coachbox](https://coachbox.app/en/compare/final-surge-review/)
- TrainingPeaks: the review found no public API discussion (device uploads and calendar sync only). — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/trainingpeaks-review/)

### Inferences
- For a free open-source coach app, the recommended integration strategy is:
  1. Ingest directly from watch-vendor APIs or FIT files (Garmin Health/Connect, COROS, Polar AccessLink, Suunto), or let users connect Intervals.icu via API key or OAuth.
  2. Treat Strava as optional and display-to-owner only.
  3. Do not feed Strava API data into LLM prompts without Strava's written approval.
- Strava's June 2026 change (a developer must hold a paid Strava subscription) adds a recurring cost and a ToS risk for a free OSS project. Per-user self-hosted API keys may also count as Standard-tier apps.
- **[BK]** Strava default rate limits have historically been 200 requests/15 min and 2,000/day overall, with lower read limits (100/15 min, 1,000/day). A single-app quota is shared across all users unless raised by Strava, which hurts a popular free app.
- **[BK]** The TrainingPeaks API requires an approved partnership application and is not available to hobby developers. Workouts can be pushed to TP only by approved partners. Garmin ownership (Jul 2026) makes future openness uncertain.
- **[BK]** Stryd exports FIT files with power data to Garmin/Strava/TrainingPeaks and so on, but has no public developer API.
- **[BK]** The Intervals.icu API is free to use for personal access via an API key in settings. Third-party apps register for OAuth. The operator (David Tinker) is broadly supportive of integrations, but heavy apps should follow the forum guidance.
- Several platforms (Strava, Intervals.icu, Runalyze) shipped MCP connectors in 2026. This "bring your own LLM" pattern is becoming the norm and could be emulated by an open-source app.

### Gaps
- The official Strava developer docs/API agreement text (2026 version) and current rate limits were not fetched. The June 2026 details rely on a single TechRepublic article (which cites TechCrunch).
- Not found: a TrainingPeaks public API policy page, Stryd API, Final Surge API, and the scope and auth of the Runalyze/SportTracks APIs.
- Whether Strava has since published guidance on inference-only LLM use (after June 2026) is unknown.
