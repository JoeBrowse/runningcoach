# Dedicated Running Training-Plan Apps (state as of 2026-11-25)

Scope: Runna (Strava-owned), Coopah, V.O2 / VDOT O2 (official Jack Daniels app), McMillan Running, TrainAsONE, Campus Coach, Run With Hal (Hal Higdon), Marathon Handbook.

Important scope note: "V.O2 (VDOT running app)" and "the official Jack Daniels / VDOT O2 app" are the **same product**. iOS lists it as "V.O2: Running Coach" by The Run SMART Project, LLC, built on Jack Daniels' methodology ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id977666802)). Android lists it as "V.O2: Running Coach and Plans" from developer "VDOT O2", package `com.runsmartproject.calendar`, on Google Play since Nov 2015 ([Google Play developer page](https://play.google.com/store/apps/developer?id=VDOT+O2); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/v-o2-running-coach-and-plans/com.runsmartproject.calendar)). It is profiled once below.

Source-quality caveats: Reddit threads did not show up in any search, so Reddit sentiment is taken second-hand, mainly from WSJ/AOL/TechRadar coverage that cites Reddit and TikTok. DC Rainmaker coverage of these apps did not turn up. the5krunner (2026 articles) is the most current independent source used. App Store listings are snapshots and some version dates show no year.

## Q1: What is the current price and what does the free tier include?

### Takeaway
Runna is the most expensive standalone app ($19.99/mo, $119.99/yr; UK rising to £17.99/£109.99 from 10 Dec 2026). The Strava bundle costs $149.99/yr. Coopah ($14.99/$79.99) and Run With Hal+ ($6.99/mo, $59.99/yr) cost less. V.O2 costs $12.99/mo or $99.99/yr, or $44.99–$99.99 per fixed plan. Marathon Handbook RunClub costs $72/yr. TrainAsONE is the only app whose free tier includes essentially all features. Most others are free to download but give you only one week or two weeks of a plan.

## Q2: How does plan generation and adaptation work (methodology)?

### Takeaway
Runna uses coach-written templates that an undisclosed algorithm tailors and adjusts. A single race-time estimate sets every pace, and paces shift after repeated misses or beats. V.O2 is pure Daniels VDOT, but adaptation is semi-manual. TrainAsONE is the most algorithmic and fully automatic, but has the most reports of odd scheduling. Hal, McMillan and Campus adjust paces automatically, but the plans are built on fixed templates.

### Cited Findings

**Runna**
- Experienced coaches design the plans, and an algorithm tailors and adapts them. Runna's 2026 statement to TechRadar is narrower than February 2026 reporting that mentioned ML and generative AI. The algorithm is unpublished — [the5krunner, Is Runna AI?](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- Inputs are current ability, experience, weekly run days, race distance and date. Volume and difficulty can be changed at any time — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- One estimated race time sets all pace targets. Runna doesn't train toward a goal time; to aim faster you raise the estimate — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- Main adaptation: pace analysis of tempo and interval sessions. Targets change after repeated misses or beats, not after one session, and outlier reps are ignored. Race predictions use data from similar runners and assume a flat course in good conditions — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- "Runna AI" lets runners review pace adjustments over time. At a May 2026 briefing, leadership said automated AI coaches cannot replicate the nuance personalised coaching needs — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/); [search summary of Runna/Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/runna-announces-five-new-product-updates-and-two-new-coaches)
- "Adapt for Heat" (29 July 2026) adjusts pace targets using forecast heat and humidity, not measured heat strain. "Not Feeling 100%" adapts training for illness, aches or limited time — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2226300/runnas-new-coaching-feature-will-adjust-your-workout-based-on-local-heat-and-humidity/)
- Early 2026 beginner plans added personalised Workout Briefings — [Runna press](https://www.runna.com/en-gb/press/runna-introduces-updated-beginner-running-plans-for-2026)
- After injury criticism, Runna started an academic review with Loughborough University (Aug 2026) covering pace progression, intensity distribution and recovery. Stanford Lifestyle Medicine is a second partner. On 12 Nov 2026 Runna lengthened the gradual return after Holiday Mode and capped one session per week by distance or time in seven-day plans — [the5krunner hub](https://the5krunner.com/runna/); [Strava press, 12 Nov 2026](https://press.strava.com/articles/runna-announces-five-new-product-updates-and-two-new-coaches)

**Coopah**
- Adaptive plans are built around your goal, current fitness and weekly schedule. Sessions can be moved, and the app tells you "what to run each day and why" — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/28/coopah-vs-runna/); [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/coopah-the-run-coaching-app/id1587165314)
- Plans update as you sync activities from Garmin, Apple Health or Strava, or enter them manually — via search summary of [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1587165314)
- It positions itself as "a coach rather than a training app", mixing human coaches with AI answers — [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/coopah-the-run-coaching-app/id1587165314)

**V.O2 / VDOT O2**
- Plans follow Jack Daniels' VDOT formulas. The app assesses fitness (VDOT) and sets training paces. There are three ways in: Adaptive Trainer, a fixed Goal Race Plan, or being matched with a private coach (two-way chat) — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id977666802)
- User complaint: adaptation is limited. Runners have to tell the app manually that they're ready to level up, and must mark completed workouts manually even though watch data already shows them — [App Store US reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id977666802)

**TrainAsONE**
- Builds a custom plan from ability, goals and data, and adjusts it continuously. It supports multiple concurrent goals from a first 5K to an ultra, adjusts automatically for missed or off-plan workouts, and gives return-to-training guidance after illness — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1541355896)
- Claims "You'll never have to worry about overtraining, junk miles, or how to taper ever again!" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1541355896)
- Described as offering the "deepest AI personalisation" (competitor blog, low reliability) — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=7041)

**McMillan**
- Plans use McMillan Calculator paces, which "automatically advance as you move toward your goal race". There are 200+ plans with strength and core routines — [McMillan](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/product/unlimited-training-plans)
- Run Team AI is powered by RunDot, whose "FitLogic" AI (from TriDot's Predictive Fitness) adjusts the next session after each run — [endurancesportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/rundot-launched-by-predictive-fitness-developer-of-tridot/); [RunDot App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6747775666)

**Campus Coach**
- Plans run from 12 weeks to a year, built on pace zones and rest periods. The subscription adjusts all programs automatically, with pace adaptation based on progress — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6446962176); [The Running Collective](https://blog.therunningcollective.fr/campus-coach-l-application-running-avis-trc/)
- "Coach analysis" is in early access (v5.12). "Fitness goals" target specific qualities for a set period (v6.4.0, Nov 2026) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6446962176)

**Run With Hal**
- Hal Higdon plans for 5K, 8K, 10K, 15K, half, marathon and 50K are "adaptive plans that adjust automatically as you train". You can set blackout dates and customise long runs, and multiple races blend into one adaptive plan — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/run-with-hal-training-plans/id1437166081)
- Developer: Peaksware, LLC — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/run-with-hal-training-plans/id1437166081)

**Marathon Handbook**
- The plans are PDF and Google Sheets files. Adaptive AI coaching from "Mara" is offered to some tiers — [Marathon Handbook Training Plan Library](https://marathonhandbook.com/training-plans/); search summary of [choose-plan](https://marathonhandbook.com/choose-plan/)

### Inferences
- Peaksware is TrainingPeaks' parent company, and the5krunner reports Garmin acquired TrainingPeaks in July 2026 ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)). So Run With Hal is probably now indirectly owned by Garmin. That last step is an inference and should be verified.
- Methodology spectrum: Daniels VDOT (V.O2) and McMillan paces are formula-based. Hal is template-based. Runna and Coopah are coach templates plus an algorithm. TrainAsONE and RunDot are ML-first.

### Gaps
- Coopah's methodology in depth: whose principles, and what the algorithm actually does.
- Exactly how TrainAsONE's model works (no whitepaper found).
- Whether Marathon Handbook's "Mara" produces adaptive plans in an app or only chat.

## Q3: What are the top complaints and top praise?

### Takeaway
Runna is praised for structure, watch integration and polish. Its main criticisms are injury risk from aggressive paces (WSJ, Feb 2026), slow support and price. Coopah is praised for human coaches and value, and criticised for clunkiness and a weak watch experience. V.O2 is praised for Daniels credibility and watch push, and criticised for manual adaptation and bugs. TrainAsONE gets mixed reviews over odd or unsafe workout choices. Hal is criticised for rigidity and taper oddities. Campus has a tiny US review base and complaints about UI and support.

### Cited Findings

**Runna**
- Feb 2026: the WSJ reported physical therapists seeing several Runna-related injury cases a week, mainly stress fractures, shin splints and Achilles tendinopathy. Cautionary tales spread on TikTok and Reddit. The problem areas were pace progression, intensity distribution and recovery — [the5krunner Feb 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/02/21/runna-ai-marathon-training-injury/); [AOL/Runner's World](https://www.aol.com/articles/inside-controversy-surrounding-strava-run-162700562.html); [the5krunner Loughborough](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/25/runna-loughborough-university-partnership/)
- Runna's head coach Steph Kessell cites a self-reported injury rate 43% below a 2015 Sports Medicine benchmark. The cohort is unspecified — [the5krunner Nov 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/11/12/quick-read-runna-plan-updates/) (via search summary)
- Trustpilot: paces are "ridiculous and NOT sustainable" in heat and humidity, the plan "went too fast too quick leading to overload and injury", and support says "they're working on it and it's months later" — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/runna.com) (via search summary)
- Praise: structure, Apple Watch integration, and suitability for beginners and people returning from injury — [Trustpilot / search summary](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/runna.com)

## Q4: What unique features does each have, and the standard-feature matrix?

### Takeaway
What sets each apart: Runna has polish, the Strava bundle, Adapt for Heat and a standalone Apple Watch app with voice coaching. Coopah has human coaches plus a money-back guarantee and a Race Day Confidence score. V.O2 has authentic Daniels VDOT plus a marketplace of human coaches. TrainAsONE has a near-free fully adaptive plan with multiple goals. Campus has trail focus and "Crews". Hal is cheap with blended multi-race plans. Marathon Handbook combines a community/media membership with an AI chat coach.

### Cited Findings — unique features

### Inferences
- No app in this group covers readiness from HRV or sleep, route planning, or proper nutrition planning well. These are gaps in the market.
- Runna is the only one that is strong on polish, broad watch support and audio together, but it is the most expensive and has the injury story attached.
- Coopah's weak spot is the in-run experience (no audio, no native watch app, imports only).

### Gaps
- TrainAsONE watch integrations (Garmin and others) could not be confirmed from accessible sources.

- Heart rate zone support and treadmill handling in V.O2, Coopah and TrainAsONE.

- Whether Marathon Handbook has a native mobile app (none found).
