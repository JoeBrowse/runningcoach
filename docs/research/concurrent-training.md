# Running and lifting in the same week (concurrent training)

This note sums up the research that sets how Skid Marks plans a week for people who run **and** lift. It was written in November 2026. Check the papers before you rely on exact numbers.

## Findings

1. **The interference is small.** Doing running and lifting together does not stop strength or muscle gains for most people. Older meta-analyses found no interference for muscle size or maximal strength, but some for power (explosive strength). A 2022 subgroup analysis found a modest loss of muscle-fibre growth, larger when the endurance work is running than cycling. A 2024 meta-analysis of 59 studies found small interference for lower-body strength in trained men, not in women and not in untrained people.
   - Sources: [Lundberg et al., Sports Medicine 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474354), [Schumann et al., meta-analysis preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/37/71/54), [Huiberts et al., Sports Medicine 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37847373/).
2. **Separate the two by time.** Interference is larger when strength and endurance work are in the same session than with a 6-hour or 24-hour gap. Common guidance: at least 6 hours between a run and a lift on the same day; 24 hours is better when one goal has priority.
   - Sources: [Schumann et al., meta-analysis preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/37/71/54), [JCU / Sports Medicine 2019 summary](https://www.jcu.edu.au/news/releases/2019/april/how-to-combine-leg-day-with-running), [Tundidor-Duque et al., Journal of Human Kinetics](https://jhk.termedia.pl/Factors-Influencing-the-Efficacy-of-Concurrent-Training-in-Team-Sports-A-Narrative,216685,0,2.html).
3. **Order on the same day: priority first.** Sources disagree. Endurance-first is advised when running is the priority; strength-first preserves strength better. Skid Marks puts the user's priority first and asks for a gap of 6 hours or more.
4. **Heavy leg work damages muscle for days.** Muscle damage from resistance training can reduce endurance performance, and its stress lasts longer than the stress of a typical easy run. Coaching guidance: no heavy legs the day before a long run or a hard run; one to two days between them.
   - Sources: [Doma et al., Sports Medicine 2019](https://portfolio.jcu.edu.au/publications/JCU165856), [Outside: how to lift and run](https://run.outsideonline.com/training/cross-training/how-to-lift-and-run-without-wrecking-either/), [Rogue: hybrid athlete plan](https://www.roguefitness.com/gb/theindex/movement-library/how-to-train-as-a-hybrid-athlete-strength-and-endurance-plan).
5. **Upper body and easy runs mix well.** Upper-body sessions interfere very little with running and can share a day with an easy run.
6. **Strength helps runners.** Strength training 2 to 3 times a week for 8 to 12 weeks improves running economy. Heavy loads (80 to 90% of 1RM or more) work better than light loads, and mixing heavy lifting with plyometrics may work best.
   - Sources: [Balsalobre-Fernández et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694507/), [Llanos-Lagos et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y), [Eihara et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9653533/).
7. **A little lifting goes a long way.** Beginners gain strength with one session a week for the first 8 to 12 weeks. Two sessions a week at moderate loads give most of the possible strength gains. Public-health guidance asks for at least 2 days a week of muscle-strengthening work.
   - Sources: [Minimalist training, narrative review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933173/), [Attia summary of the frequency meta-analysis](https://peterattiamd.com/lowering-the-barrier-to-entry-for-resistance-training/).

## Rules that Skid Marks uses

These rules come from the findings above. Rules 6 and 7 are common coaching practice, not tested in the cited papers.

1. Heavy legs counts as a hard day. No heavy legs within 48 hours before a quality run or 72 hours before the long run or a race.
2. Upper body can share a day with an easy run.
3. A hard run and a heavy leg session can share a day only when the user allows two sessions a day: the priority first, then a gap of 6 hours or more. This keeps the next day easy.
4. Two lift sessions a week is the default for people who run. One is enough for beginners. Three or four only when lifting is the priority.
5. Lift split by lift days: 1 or 2 days: full body. 3 days: upper, lower, upper (one leg day). 4 days: upper, lower, upper, lower (lifting priority only).
6. At least one full rest day a week. Beginners get at least two.
7. Easy days stay easy. On a day with two sessions, both are easy, or one is the priority session and the other is easy.
8. Near an A race: maintenance lifting in the last 10 days, no heavy lifting in race week.
9. An unplanned session changes the plan. An unplanned heavy leg session or hard run eases the next hard session in the following 48 hours. An unplanned easy session counts towards the week, and the next easy run can get shorter.

## Starting weeks (defaults the user can change)

| Level | Runs | Lifts | Training days | Rest days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 3 (run/walk) | 1 to 2 (full body) | 4 to 5 | 2 to 3 |
| Intermediate | 3 to 4 | 2 to 3 | 5 to 6 | 1 to 2 |
| Advanced | 4 to 6 | 2 to 4 | 6 (doubles allowed) | 1 |
