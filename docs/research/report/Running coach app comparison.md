# Running apps charge more, adapt too little

Running coach apps in late 2026 sell six things: race plans, plan adaptation, coaching (AI or human), in-run guidance, watch sync and health metrics. Prices go from free to $28.99 a month. Free apps include Nike Run Club, NHS Couch to 5K, Intervals.icu and the TrainAsONE core. Most dedicated plan apps and AI coaches cost $15 to $20 a month. Few of them have a real free tier. No single app does everything well. Runna leads on polish and watch support, but it costs the most and has an injury controversy. Watch brands give good plans free, but only on their own hardware. Mass-market apps give free audio coaching, but their plans do not adapt. Three gaps stay open. Runners want free adaptive plans, a safe return after injury or a break, and data that they own. A free app can fill these gaps with a local-first design. It must not use trademarked names such as TSS and VDOT. It must not depend on the Strava or Garmin cloud APIs.

## Six feature groups and a paid AI layer define the 2026 market

All coach apps start with a goal-race plan. The methods differ a lot. V.O2 uses Jack Daniels' VDOT formulas, and McMillan uses its own calculator paces ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id977666802); [McMillan](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/product/unlimited-training-plans)). Run With Hal uses fixed templates. Runna uses coach-written templates and an unpublished algorithm. One race-time estimate sets all Runna paces ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)). AI Endurance trains a neural network for each athlete every 24 hours ([SensAI, competitor review](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/ai-endurance-review-2026)). Athletica uses a modified Banister model and says it is "absolutely not a black box" ([Athletica forum](https://forum.athletica.ai/t/athletica-ai-review-after-marathon-training-block-two-thumbs-down/4904)).

Big companies now own key apps. **Strava bought Runna in April 2025.** The two apps still have separate subscriptions. A joint bundle costs $149.99 a year ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/28/runna-vs-strava/)). Strava stopped new plan creation on its own website, so plans now come from Runna ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/14/is-runna-free/)). **Garmin bought TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic on 22 July 2026** ([Bicycle Retailer](https://bicycleretailer.com/industry-news/2026/07/22/garmin-acquires-trainingpeaks-trainheroic-platforms)). This makes Garmin the owner of the TSS trademark and the main coach platform.

Base coaching stays free on most watches. Conversational AI is becoming a separate paid layer. Garmin Connect+ costs $6.99 a month, but it has no chat coach ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/11/08/quick-read-garmin-ai-coaches/)). Suunto's chat AI Coach becomes paid in January 2027 ([ActionHub](https://www.actionhub.com/news/2026/10/21/understanding-what-suunto-ai-coach-can-do/)). Google's Gemini coach needs Google Health Premium at $9.99 a month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/googles-9-99-per-month-ai-health-coach-launches-may-19/)). COROS takes a different path. Its official MCP lets runners connect their own Claude or ChatGPT ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/05/13/coros-mcp-ai-data/)). Strava, Intervals.icu, Runalyze and AI Endurance also added MCP links in 2026. "Bring your own AI" is now a common pattern.

Prices are going up and free tiers are going down. **Runna's beginner plans became paid on 5 June 2026** ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/14/is-runna-free/)). Runna's UK prices rise about 10% from 10 December 2026 ([Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/18298149-changes-to-our-uk-subscription)). Garmin Connect+ caused a large backlash at launch ([DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2025/04/garmin-connect-plus-fallout-dumpster-fire.html)). Some features are weak in almost every app. These are recovery-aware periodisation, injury return, nutrition and route planning inside plan apps. AI planners also lack audio cues and phone GPS.

## Per-app insights: what each app does well, misses, and keeps

### Dedicated plan apps sell structure, but adapt too fast or too slow

**Runna** (Strava-owned since April 2025)
- **Does well:** Runna gives polished plans from a first 5K to an ultra. It pushes workouts to Garmin, Apple, COROS, Suunto and Amazfit watches ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/runna/)). Its own Apple Watch app gives voice coaching and standalone GPS ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/11/12/runna-apple-watch/)). "Adapt for Heat" changes pace targets from the heat and humidity forecast ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2226300/runnas-new-coaching-feature-will-adjust-your-workout-based-on-local-heat-and-humidity/)).
- **Could do better:** In February 2026 the WSJ reported physical therapists seeing several Runna-linked injuries a week ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/02/21/runna-ai-marathon-training-injury/)). Runna started a review with Loughborough University. On 12 November 2026 it capped one session a week and lengthened the return after Holiday Mode ([Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/runna-announces-five-new-product-updates-and-two-new-coaches)). One reviewer said the return-after-injury plan never asked what the injury was ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/runna.com)). Runna is the most expensive standalone plan app.
- **Runners would miss:** step-by-step watch workouts with adaptive paces, Apple Watch voice coaching, strength sessions inside the plan, and the Strava link.

**Coopah**
- **Does well:** Coopah gives access to real human coaches. Coaches typically reply within an hour ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/coopah-app-review-an-ideal-reasonably-priced-running-companion-app)). Every plan includes strength and conditioning. It offers a Race Day Confidence Score and a money-back guarantee. It has 4.8/5 from 1.4k UK ratings ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/coopah-the-run-coaching-app/id1587165314)).
- **Could do better:** TechRadar found no guidance while you run. Coopah has no native watch app and only imports runs. It has no treadmill option. Moving one workout does not shift the others. One trial user never reached a human coach ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/28/coopah-vs-runna/)).
- **Runners would miss:** a human coach to message after illness or injury, and the strength and yoga videos.

**V.O2 (VDOT O2, official Jack Daniels app)**
- **Does well:** V.O2 is the official Daniels method. It pushes workouts to Garmin, COROS and Apple Watch. It has 4.9/5 from 6.3K US ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id977666802)). Runners can also hire a private coach in the app.
- **Could do better:** Adaptation is partly manual. Runners must level up by hand and mark finished workouts by hand. Garmin runs attach to the latest planned workout with no undo. Reviewers report "big bugs".
- **Runners would miss:** Daniels paces on the watch and the builder for complex intervals with audio cues.

**McMillan Running**
- **Does well:** McMillan offers 200+ plans. Paces come from the McMillan Calculator and advance as you near your race. Plans include strength and core work ([McMillan](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/product/unlimited-training-plans)). The new Run Team AI uses RunDot's "FitLogic" to adjust the next session after each run ([endurancesportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/rundot-launched-by-predictive-fitness-developer-of-tridot/)).
- **Could do better:** Pricing is confusing. Several undated pages show different tiers. The notes could not verify the app listing, watch brands or Run Team AI page. No independent user reviews were found.
- **Runners would miss:** the large plan library tied to McMillan paces, and access to McMillan coaches.

**TrainAsONE**
- **Does well:** TrainAsONE has the most generous free tier. "All features are available without a subscription" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1541355896)). Plans adapt automatically. Runners can set several goals at once. It gives return-to-training guidance after illness.
- **Could do better:** Reviews report unsafe schedules. One runner got a 59-minute run two days after an 11-minute run after injury. Another got a 13.6-mile long run three weeks before a marathon. Most reviews are from 2021–22. It has 3.9/5 from only 47 ratings. Its watch support is unverified.
- **Runners would miss:** automatic adaptation without paying.

**Campus Coach**
- **Does well:** Campus covers road and trail, with plans from 12 weeks to a year. It has a race catalogue and "Crews" groups. It exports workouts to Garmin, Apple, COROS and Suunto ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6446962176)). It shipped five releases between late October and November 2026.
- **Could do better:** It costs $19.99 a month, the same as Runna. It has only 16 US ratings, with complaints about the UI and support. The free version shows only two weeks of the plan ([The Running Collective](https://blog.therunningcollective.fr/campus-coach-l-application-running-avis-trc/)).
- **Runners would miss:** trail plans, Crews and the zone and heart-rate breakdowns.

**Run With Hal**
- **Does well:** Run With Hal+ is the cheapest paid plan app at $59.99 a year. It carries the Hal Higdon brand. It blends several races into one plan and accepts blackout dates. It has 4.7/5 from 2.8K ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/run-with-hal-training-plans/id1437166081)).
- **Could do better:** Users report rigid schedules and no heat adjustment. Mileage can peak the week before the race. Cross-training is logged as a run. Many 2025–26 releases fixed purchase bugs. The developer is Peaksware. Ownership after the Garmin–TrainingPeaks deal is unclear; verify it.
- **Runners would miss:** the "Hal says" daily tips and plans that fit a busy schedule.

**Marathon Handbook**
- **Does well:** RunClub costs $72 a year. It includes 80+ plans from 5K to 100 miles, community rooms, a podcast and 14+ hours of courses ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/choose-plan/)). A free tier gives beginner plans and calculators.
- **Could do better:** Plans are PDF and Google Sheets files. The notes found no native app and no watch sync. Tier names conflict between pages ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/training-plans/)). The scope of the "Mara" AI coach is unclear.
- **Runners would miss:** the community, the AMAs and the course library.

### AI coaches adapt well on paper, but rely on watches and lack audio

**HumanGO**
- **Does well:** HumanGO covers the widest range of sports. Its AI coach "Hugo" re-plans from workouts, heart rate, sleep, fatigue and schedule. ## Implications for a free running coach app

### Must-have features

A free app must match the basics that runners now expect. The table lists each feature and the evidence for it.

| Must-have feature | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Goal-race plans from run/walk to marathon | Runna made beginner plans paid in June 2026. NHS Couch to 5K has one fixed plan. |
| Conservative automatic adaptation | Too-rigid or too-aggressive adaptation is the top complaint across Runna, Hal, TrainAsONE, V.O2 and AI Endurance. |
| Missed-run, illness, holiday and injury handling | Runna's injury plan did not ask about the injury. Stryd and TrainAsONE show that body-status re-planning is possible. |
| Plan seeded from past runs | Garmin Coach ignores years of history ([ShouldITrain](https://www.shoulditrain.com/blog/garmin-coach-review)). |
| Phone GPS recording with audio cues | NRC and NHS win on audio. AI planners have no audio. |
| Structured workouts on the watch | Runners cite this as the feature they would miss in Runna and V.O2. |
| Pace zones, heart-rate zones and race prediction | All serious apps include them. Use neutral names (see below). |
| Fitness, fatigue and form chart | Most platforms use this Banister-type model. The model is published and free to use. |
| Basic strength and mobility | Runna, Coopah and Kotcha include it in plans. |
| Treadmill mode | Coopah users complain when runs import as outdoor. |
| Full data export (FIT, GPX, TCX, CSV) | Runners fear lock-in after the Strava and Garmin changes. |

### Gaps a free app can fill

**A free adaptive plan is rare.** Only TrainAsONE gives full adaptation free, and its reviews raise safety issues. A free app wins on price by default.

**Nobody combines periodisation, daily readiness and plain explanation.** Watch plans are either reactive (Garmin) or general fitness (Polar, Samsung). The watch notes call this combination "open". A free app can build, peak and taper, then adjust each day from readiness data.

**Safe return is under-served.** A free app can ask what the injury is and how long the break was. It can then rebuild load slowly. Runna added a weekly session cap only in November 2026. Transparent safety caps can be a feature, not a fix.

**Women's health is weak everywhere.** Garmin gives cycle guidance only. Wild.AI is paid and feels static ([Canadian Running](https://runningmagazine.ca/?p=84720)). A free app can offer optional, on-device cycle and symptom logging. The evidence for phase-based training was not researched, so adjust by symptoms, not by phase.

**Audio plus adaptation is missing.** AI planners adapt but give no in-run audio. Audio apps give audio but do not adapt. One app can do both.

**Cross-brand support is patchy.** Runna skips Polar and Wear OS. Samsung's coach needs a Galaxy Watch 8. A file-based app works with any device that exports FIT or writes to a health store.

**No mobile open-source coach exists.** Open-source apps record runs (OpenTracks, FitoTrack) or bridge devices (Gadgetbridge). Open-source coaches such as OpenRun need Docker or a server and are early-stage ([GitHub](https://github.com/mandel2000/openrun)). No polished, offline coach is in the app stores.

**Bring-your-own AI is optional, not core.** Strava, COROS, Intervals.icu and Runalyze use MCP. A free app can expose its own data the same way. The plan maths must stay in a rules engine. OpenRun already keeps pace maths out of the LLM.

### Legal and technical limits

| Item | Status in late 2026 | What a free app should do |
|---|---|---|
| **TSS, Training Stress Score, Normalized Power, Intensity Factor** | "TSS" is a live USPTO mark (serial 85913859) of TrainingPeaks, now Garmin-owned ([uspto.report](https://uspto.report/TM/85913859)). Commentary says the marks cover names, not formulas ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/07/22/garmin-acquires-trainingpeaks-what-changes/)). This is not legal advice. | Use "training load", "fitness / fatigue / form". Never use the marked names. |
| **VDOT, V.O2** | "VDOT" (serial 88045498) is listed as registered to The Run SMART Project. "VDOT O2" is listed as dead from 31 Oct 2025. "V.O2" is pending ([TrademarkDir](https://trademarkdir.com/trademark/vdot-88045498); [Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/v-o2-99410979)). These come from secondary sites. Verify on USPTO TSDR. | Use a neutral name such as "running fitness score". Cite Daniels and Gilbert's published equations. Do not imply endorsement. |
| **Strava API** | Since Nov 2024: show data only to its owner, and no use in AI models ([DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/11/stravas-changes-to-kill-off-apps.html)). From June 2026: Standard-tier developers need a paid Strava subscription. Intermediary platforms are not supported. AI users must "stop and seek written guidance" ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-strava-api-scraping-crackdown/)). The June details come from one secondary source. | Make Strava optional. Show Strava data only to the owner. Never send Strava data to an LLM. Expect a recurring developer fee. |
| **Garmin Connect Developer Program** | Free to approved businesses. It is the only way to push workouts to Garmin ([Garmin](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/training-api)). New applications paused in spring 2026 ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/14/garmin-developer-api-access-paused/)). An October report says it reopened and added a read-only personal API ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/22/garmin-personal-api/)). The reports conflict. | Do not depend on it. Use FIT file import and export, a Connect IQ app, or an Intervals.icu bridge. Do not use unofficial Garmin libraries; they break and risk the terms. |
| **Apple HealthKit and WorkoutKit** | Free, on-device. WorkoutKit lets apps schedule custom workouts on Apple Watch* (iOS 17+). The notes did not research the terms. Privacy and App Review rules apply. | Read runs from HealthKit. Push workouts with WorkoutKit. Check Apple's health-data rules before release. |
| **Android Health Connect** | A coach can read Galaxy Watch runs through Health Connect without Strava ([therunninggenie, commercial](https://therunninggenie.com/blog/best-ai-running-coach-for-samsung-galaxy-watch-users)). Pushing workouts to Samsung or Pixel watches is not openly supported*. Terms were not researched. | Read from Health Connect. Offer phone audio guidance for Android watch users. |
| **FIT files** | The notes could not retrieve the FIT SDK licence. Background knowledge says the "FIT Protocol License" allows free use, including open source*. Garmin's cookbook explains workout FIT files. | Import FIT, GPX and TCX. Export workout FIT files. Verify the licence at developer.garmin.com/fit first. |
| **Polar, COROS, Suunto, Google APIs** | Polar AccessLink is open to any user but read-focused*. COROS and Suunto push needs a partner deal; COROS has a self-service MCP route. Google Health API scopes are all "Restricted" and need a security review ([Google](https://developers.google.com/health/about)). | Use AccessLink for Polar reads. Treat partner APIs as later add-ons. |
| **Load ratio as injury predictor** | Studies call the acute:chronic workload ratio "an inaccurate metric" ([BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/15/921.full); [Impellizzeri 2021](https://iris.univr.it/retrieve/e34cfb98-e922-4c2f-aab5-18583ab7e31b/Impellizzeri_What%20Role%20Do%20Chronic%20Workloads%20Play_SportMed_2021.pdf)). One study found the effects trivial ([Coyne et al.](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/7321/)). | Show load trends as information. Do not claim to predict injury. |
| **Plan content and health law** | Copying Hal Higdon, NHS or Pfitzinger plan tables is likely restricted, but this is unverified. GDPR health-data rules and medical-device rules were not researched. | Generate plans from rules, not copied tables. Get legal advice before any injury claim. |

### Suggested architecture

Build the app local-first. Keep all data on the phone by default. The runner owns the data as standard files. This avoids lock-in and cuts server cost to almost zero.

| Layer | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Storage | On-device database. Optional end-to-end encrypted sync. | Runners fear lock-in. Local data needs no server. |
| Input | FIT, GPX and TCX import. HealthKit on iOS. Health Connect on Android. Optional Intervals.icu API key. Optional OpenTracks and Gadgetbridge links. | These routes are free and do not depend on Strava or Garmin cloud access. |
| Plan engine | Deterministic rules. Published methods: Riegel prediction, Daniels-type pace equations under a neutral name, Banister load, heart-rate zones, about 80/20 intensity, a taper. Hard caps on weekly increase and on long-run length. | Rules are transparent and testable. Athletica gets praise for "not a black box". The formulas are flagged as background knowledge in the notes; cite primary papers. |
| Adaptation | Re-plan after missed runs, illness, injury, holidays and readiness changes. Ask questions on return. | This is the top unmet need. |
| Recording | Phone GPS with audio cues. Bluetooth heart-rate and footpod sensors. Treadmill mode. | Audio is the main reason runners love NRC and NHS Couch to 5K. |
| Output | WorkoutKit for Apple Watch. Workout FIT files for Garmin, COROS and others. A Connect IQ app later. Calendar (.ics) export. | These avoid the paused Garmin business API. |
| AI (optional) | On-device model or bring-your-own LLM through MCP. The LLM explains; the engine calculates. Never use Strava data. | It avoids LLM arithmetic errors and the Strava AI ban. |

### A sustainable free model

Keep all coaching free. Do not show ads. Do not sell data. Intervals.icu shows one working model: a free core and an optional $4 Supporter tier ([Intervals.icu](https://www.intervals.icu/pricing)). Its donations are small, at about £926 on Open Collective, so most money comes from subscriptions ([Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/intervalsicu)). Endurain shows another tool. Its code is AGPL-3.0, but its name is trademarked, so nobody can sell hosting under its brand ([Endurain](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/endurain-project/endurain/master/README.md)). OpenTracks makes privacy the product: no internet access, no ads and no analytics ([GitHub](https://github.com/OpenTracksApp/OpenTracks)).

A free coach app can combine these. Use a copyleft licence and a protected name. Offer an optional supporter tier for extras that cost money to run, such as hosted sync. Never put plans, adaptation or safety features behind it. A local-first design keeps running costs low, so a small supporter base can fund the project. The notes did not research public-health or grant funding, such as the NHS model behind Couch to 5K.

## Conclusion

The market is splitting in two. Race plans are becoming a free feature of watches and of Garmin's own platform. Paid apps now sell polish, human contact or AI explanation on top. At the same time, Strava and Garmin control more of the data and limit what other apps can do with it. This raises the value of a neutral, file-based coach that works with any device. It also means a free app cannot rely on any one company's cloud.

The biggest opportunity is trust, not more metrics. Runners complain most about injury risk, rigid adaptation, price rises and hard cancellation. A free app with transparent rules, conservative caps and data the runner owns answers all four. The main risk is safety. A free app that harms runners will lose trust as fast as Runna did. Safety caps and honest limits must be core features from the first release.
